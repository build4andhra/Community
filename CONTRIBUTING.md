# Contributing to Build4Andhra

You can contribute by writing code, testing, documenting, translating, teaching, mentoring, designing, researching, organizing events, reviewing issues, or helping newcomers.

## Ways to contribute

- Improve an existing project or fix an issue.
- Create a tutorial, learning note, example, or workshop.
- Start or join a study group.
- Review code, documentation, accessibility, or usability.
- Propose a project, experiment, reading group, or community event.
- Help with triage, design, translations, testing, or onboarding.

## Before you begin

- Read `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, `GOVERNANCE.md`, `LICENSING.md`, and `SECURITY.md`.
- Check the repository's license and contribution requirements.
- Confirm you have the right to submit your work.
- Do not submit employer-owned, client-owned, university-restricted, government-confidential, or third-party material without permission.
- Never include secrets, credentials, private keys, personal data, or confidential logs.

## Workflow

1. Search existing issues and pull requests.
2. Open an issue for substantial work and discuss scope first.
3. Fork the repository and create a focused branch.
4. Make a small, reviewable change.
5. Run relevant tests or checks and document the results.
6. Update docs and examples where needed.
7. Submit a pull request explaining the purpose, changes, tests, and risks.
8. Respond constructively to feedback.

## Learning-friendly reviews

We aim to make contributions educational. Reviewers should explain requested changes clearly and respectfully. Contributors are encouraged to ask questions, request context, and share what they learned. A first contribution does not need to be large or perfect.

## Contribution rights and licensing

By submitting work, you confirm to the best of your knowledge that you created it or have permission to contribute it, and that you have identified relevant third-party code, assets, datasets, and license notices. Your contribution will be distributed under the license stated in the repository unless a separate written agreement approved by the project says otherwise. This does not transfer your copyright by itself. Any CLA or DCO requirement must be clearly documented before contributions are accepted.

## AI-assisted contributions

AI tools may be used as aids, but contributors remain responsible for submitted work. Review generated code and content, test it, check provenance and licenses where possible, and disclose material AI assistance when required by project policy or when it affects safety, evaluation, or authorship. Do not submit confidential material to AI services without authorization.

## Data and research

Document dataset sources, access terms, licenses, limitations, and collection methods. Do not republish data merely because it is publicly visible. Do not publish personal, sensitive, confidential, or unlawfully obtained data. Obtain required consent, institutional approvals, and permissions for research involving people.

## Security-sensitive changes

Do not publish exploitable vulnerability details in public issues. Follow `SECURITY.md`.

Maintainers may request changes or decline work outside project scope, capacity, or safety requirements.
